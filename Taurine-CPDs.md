Producing **taurine-derived carbonized polymer dots (Taurine-CPDs)** involves a straightforward, bottom-up hydrothermal synthesis. Because taurine provides a rich supply of both amine ($NH_2$) and sulfonate ($SO_3^-$) groups, it acts as an exceptional co-precursor for nitrogen and sulfur co-doping.

---

### ### Required Materials & Equipment

* **Carbon/Acid Source:** Citric acid monohydrate (provides the primary carbon skeleton and carboxylic acid groups).
* **Heteroatom Source:** Taurine powder ($\text{C}_2\text{H}_7\text{NO}_3\text{S}$) (provides nitrogen and sulfur).
* **Solvent:** Ultra-pure deionized (DI) water.
* **Reaction Vessel:** Teflon-lined stainless steel hydrothermal autoclave reactor (typically 50 mL or 100 mL capacity).
* **Heating Source:** Programmable laboratory oven or muffle furnace.
* **Purification Supplies:** Dialysis tubing (molecular weight cutoff: 500–1000 Da) and $0.22\,\mu\text{m}$ syringe filters.

---

### ### Step-by-Step Synthesis Procedure

#### 1. Solution Preparation

* Dissolve **1.0 g of citric acid** and **0.5 g to 1.0 g of taurine** in **10 mL to 20 mL of deionized water** inside a small beaker.
* Stir the mixture thoroughly using a magnetic stirrer at room temperature until both precursors are completely dissolved, forming a clear, homogenous solution.

#### 2. Hydrothermal Carbonization

* Transfer the clear solution into the **Teflon-lined chamber** of the stainless steel autoclave, ensuring the liquid fills roughly 60–80% of the total volume to allow safe thermal expansion.
* Seal the autoclave tightly, place it inside a laboratory oven, and heat it to a temperature between **160°C and 200°C** (commonly optimized around **180°C**).
* Maintain this temperature for **4 to 12 hours** (typically 6 hours is sufficient to drive complete carbonization and cross-linking).

#### 3. Cooling and Initial Filtration

* Once the reaction time concludes, turn off the oven and allow the autoclave to cool down naturally to room temperature (do not force-cool with water to avoid pressure vessel stress).
* Open the autoclave and collect the dark brown/yellowish transparent aqueous product.
* Pass the crude solution through a **$0.22\,\mu\text{m}$ membrane syringe filter** to remove any large, insoluble agglomerates or soot particles.

#### 4. Purification via Dialysis

* Transfer the filtered solution into a **dialysis bag** (500–1000 Da MWCO).
* Dialyze against a large reservoir of deionized water for **24 to 48 hours**, replacing the external water periodically (every 6–8 hours) to completely remove unreacted citric acid, free taurine, and small molecular byproducts.

#### 5. Storage

* The purified Taurine-CPD solution can be stored as a stable aqueous dispersion at 4°C, or freeze-dried (lyophilized) into a solid powder for long-term preservation.

---

### ### Expected Properties

* **Optical Traits:** Strong blue or green fluorescence under UV light (typically excited around 360–420 nm).
* **Surface Chemistry:** Heavily decorated with hydrophilic carboxyl, amine, and sulfonate groups, yielding exceptional water solubility and a negative zeta potential.
**2. Carbonization**

Subject the precursor mixture to controlled hydrothermal/solvothermal heating. During this stage, dehydration, condensation and partial carbonization produce nanoscale carbonized domains.

Conceptually:

**polymer precursor + sulfur precursor → polymeric intermediate → partially carbonized S-containing dot**

The resulting sulfur may occur as different chemical environments, including **thiol, sulfide, disulfide, sulfoxide/sulfone, or oxidized sulfur groups**, depending on the precursor and reaction conditions.

**3. Cooling and purification**

After cooling, remove large particles and insoluble material using an appropriately sized membrane or centrifugation.

This step is particularly important for sulfur systems because the final suspension can contain **unreacted thiourea/cysteine, inorganic sulfur species and low-molecular-weight reaction products** that are not part of the carbon dots themselves.

**4. Dialysis / separation**

Dialysis or another validated separation technique can remove small molecular species and free sulfur-containing ions.

**5. Characterization**

Before assigning the product as an S-carbonized polymer dot, characterize it with techniques such as:

* **TEM/AFM:** particle size and morphology
* **DLS:** hydrodynamic diameter
* **XPS:** S chemical states and surface composition
* **FTIR:** surface functional groups
* **Raman:** carbon structure/disorder
* **UV–Vis/photoluminescence:** optical properties
* **zeta potential:** surface charge
* **ICP-MS/OES:** total sulfur and residual metals
* **TGA:** polymer/carbon composition
