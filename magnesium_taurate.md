Here's the general approach, based on how magnesium taurate is typically made from these reagents:

**The idea:** You can't react MgSO₄ directly with taurine to get a clean product, because the sulfate ion stays in solution and contaminates your final salt. So the trick is to first convert the magnesium into a form that reacts cleanly with taurine and releases a gas (which leaves the system) — that's where the potassium carbonate comes in.

**Step 1 – Make magnesium carbonate**
Dissolve MgSO₄ in water and K₂CO₃ in a separate portion of water. Mix them together:

MgSO₄ + K₂CO₃ → MgCO₃↓ + K₂SO₄

MgCO₃ is poorly soluble and precipitates out as a white solid. K₂SO₄ stays dissolved.

**Step 2 – Isolate the magnesium carbonate**
Filter the precipitate and wash it thoroughly with distilled water several times to rinse away the dissolved potassium sulfate. This step matters — leftover K₂SO₄ will end up in your final product otherwise.

**Step 3 – React with taurine**
Add the washed MgCO₃ to a solution of taurine in warm water, with gentle heating and stirring:

MgCO₃ + 2 Taurine → Magnesium Taurate + CO₂↑ + H₂O

The CO₂ bubbles off, driving the reaction forward. Taurine's sulfonic acid group is the part that coordinates with the Mg²⁺.

**Step 4 – Isolate the product**
Filter off any unreacted MgCO₃ (use a slight excess of taurine, or vice versa, and filter after full reaction), then evaporate the filtrate slowly (low heat or open air) to crystallize the magnesium taurate. Dry the crystals.

**Practical notes:**
- Use distilled/deionized water throughout to avoid introducing other ions.
- Taurine is only moderately soluble in cold water, so mild heating helps it dissolve and speeds the reaction.
- Confirm the reaction is essentially complete by checking that CO₂ evolution has stopped and no more solid MgCO₃ remains undissolved.

One caveat worth flagging: if the end goal is something you intend to ingest, purity and grade of your starting reagents matter a lot — technical or lab-grade MgSO₄ and K₂CO₃ aren't necessarily food-safe, and a home setup won't verify heavy-metal contamination or exact composition the way a supplement manufacturer would. If it's just for chemistry/educational interest, none of that is a concern.
